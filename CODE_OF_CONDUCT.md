# StormATC community code of conduct

StormATC should be a place where flight-simulation enthusiasts, developers, designers, testers,
writers, and first-time open-source contributors can build something useful together.

## Our standards

We expect everyone participating in issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other
project spaces to:

- Be respectful, patient, and specific.
- Welcome questions and explain unfamiliar terms without talking down to people.
- Critique ideas and code rather than the person who proposed them.
- Assume good intent while still taking security, privacy, originality, and simulation safety
  seriously.
- Give credit for ideas and work.
- Accept a maintainer's decision to stop a discussion after the relevant arguments have been
  heard.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include harassment, insults, threats, sexualised attention,
discrimination, publishing private information, deliberately exposing credentials, sustained
disruption, and pressuring someone to share personal or financial information.

## Aviation and product discussions

People have different accents, aviation backgrounds, simulator experience, and views about
realism. Those differences are valuable. Phraseology disagreements should be supported with a
public standard or a reproducible simulation example, not appeals to status.

Do not use the project to promote unsafe real-world aviation advice or attack competing products
and their communities.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, or remove content; close discussions; reject contributions; or
temporarily or permanently restrict participation when behaviour harms the community.
Enforcement decisions should be proportionate, documented privately, and made without public
retaliation.

Report urgent conduct problems privately to the repository owner using the contact information on
their GitHub profile. Do not open a public issue containing personal information. Security
vulnerabilities follow the separate process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

No code contribution is important enough to excuse abusive behaviour.
